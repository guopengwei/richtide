# Cloudflare architecture qualification

**Question:** Does the v3 RichTide architecture remain feasible on current Cloudflare Workers, Durable Objects with SQLite, D1, R2, Workflows, Queues, and Containers?

**Finding:** **Conditionally qualified.** There is no current platform-contract blocker to the proposed Cloudflare-native design on a Workers Paid account. The core authority split is sound: a per-user SQLite-backed Durable Object is the mutable credit/access authority; D1 is a rebuildable query projection; R2 holds large immutable-by-application artifacts; Workflows coordinates durable multi-step generation; Containers run stateless Python computation; and Workers enforce the external trust boundary. Workers, SQLite-backed Durable Objects, D1, R2, Workflows, Queues, and Containers are all generally available as of this review. D1 **read replication**, Workers tracing/OTel export, and Python Workflows remain beta features and must not be required for launch correctness. Workflows itself became GA in April 2025; Queues became GA in September 2024; Containers became GA in April 2026. ([Workflows release notes](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/changelog/), [Queues changelog](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/platform/changelog/), [Containers GA announcement](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-04-13-containers-sandbox-ga/), [D1 release notes](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/release-notes/), [Durable Objects overview](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/))

Qualification is subject to the corrections and production gates below. The most important are:

1. Cloudflare provides retries and at-least-once delivery, **not idempotency of RichTide's external effects**. Every Workflow step, Queue consumer, alarm, publication, billing effect, access grant, and settlement must carry a stable operation key and be independently replay-safe.
2. Containers are not inherently a sealed private backend: Internet egress is allowed by default, disk is ephemeral, cold starts are normal, and Worker/container deploys are not transactional. RichTide must deny container Internet egress by default, broker permitted access through trusted Worker outbound handlers, keep the container stateless, and deploy backward/forward-compatible Worker and image versions.
3. R2's consistency is strong, but “immutable” is an application/retention policy, not the default. Artifact keys must be unique/content-addressed, writes must be conditional or retention-locked where appropriate, and aliases must stay outside immutable objects.
4. The v3 specification prices “Queue” usage but assigns Queues no responsibility. Either remove Queues from the architecture/cost model or explicitly use them only as at-least-once transport for projection/notification outbox events; they must never become a credit, entitlement, artifact, or job authority.
5. One D1 database is single-threaded and permanently capped at 10 GB. Launch requires measured query/index cost plus an explicit partition/archive trigger before that database becomes a throughput or storage bottleneck.

Official documentation was accessed **2026-08-20**. Prices are current documentation values, not contractual quotes; recheck them and the account's commercial terms before launch.

## Contract qualification by primitive

### Workers

Workers are appropriate for the BFF and trust boundary, not for the Generation Event itself. The current paid contract gives a request 128 MB memory, 10,000 subrequests, six simultaneous outbound connections, and up to 5 minutes configured CPU, but 30 seconds is the default. HTTP wall time has no fixed maximum only while the client remains connected; runtime updates can terminate very long requests after a grace period, and post-response `waitUntil()` is capped at 30 seconds. Queue consumer and alarm invocations have a 15-minute wall limit. All generation, settlement fan-out, and retry work must therefore leave the browser request before success is acknowledged. ([Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/))

Workers Standard billing is request count plus active CPU: 10 million requests and 30 million CPU-ms included monthly, then $0.30/million requests and $0.02/million CPU-ms; the Paid account minimum is $5/month. Subrequests are not separately billed as Worker requests, but the bound service can bill its own dimension. Put an explicit `limits.cpu_ms` in every Worker, Workflow, Queue consumer and Durable Object definition rather than relying on platform maxima. ([Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/))

Operationally, Workers exposes request/error/CPU/wall-time metrics, stored logs, traces and OTLP export. Stored logs have seven-day retention on Paid, 20 million events/month included, then $0.60/million; logs over 256 KB truncate. Tracing and OTLP export are beta and begin billed usage on the dates stated by current docs, so correctness and incident reconstruction cannot depend solely on them. Persist business correlation IDs and audit facts in RichTide stores, and export redacted operational telemetry to the approved long-retention sink. ([Workers observability](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/observability/), [Workers Logs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/observability/logs/workers-logs/), [traces](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/observability/traces/), [OTel export](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/observability/exporting-opentelemetry-data/))

### SQLite-backed Durable Objects

The v3 per-user Usage Account is strongly aligned with Cloudflare's guidance: one Durable Object per logical unit needing strong coordination, SQLite-backed private storage, and a stateless Worker in front. Input/output gates and local storage transactions protect SQLite operations, but awaiting `fetch()`, R2, D1, Queue or another external call allows interleaving; those calls cannot be treated as part of the local atomic commit. The v3 rule that provider/data/LLM calls stay outside Usage Account transactions is therefore mandatory. Write the ledger mutation, state transition and outbox row in one local transaction; drain the outbox later with stable event IDs. ([Rules of Durable Objects](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/best-practices/rules-of-durable-objects/))

An object does not automatically retry client calls. Retry only errors marked `retryable`, with exponential backoff and a fresh stub, and only for idempotent RPCs. Do not retry errors marked `overloaded`; shed/load-delay instead. Every RPC that can mutate value needs a caller-generated idempotency key persisted under a uniqueness constraint before returning success. ([Durable Object error handling](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/best-practices/error-handling/))

Alarms are at-least-once, allow only one scheduled alarm per object, and automatically retry a thrown handler at most six times with exponential backoff beginning at two seconds. The proposed grant-expiration and reconciliation scheduler must therefore keep a local schedule table, make handlers replay-safe, and explicitly set a future alarm before exhausting the platform retries. An alarm is a wake-up mechanism, not a forever-retrying job queue. ([Alarms API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/alarms/))

On Paid, Durable Objects bill requests and wall-clock compute while active/non-hibernatable, plus SQLite rows read, rows written, and GB-month. Included amounts are 1 million requests, 400,000 GB-s duration, 25 billion rows read, 50 million rows written and 5 GB-month; overages are currently $0.15/million requests, $12.50/million GB-s, $0.001/million rows read, $1/million rows written and $0.20/GB-month. Model high-frequency progress polling/WebSockets and outbox scans because they affect both requests/duration and row billing. ([Durable Objects pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/))

Durable Object jurisdiction can be fixed to `eu`, `us`, or `fedramp`; ordinary location hints are best effort and only affect initial placement, and existing objects do not currently relocate dynamically. The object's ID can appear outside the jurisdiction for billing/debugging. Choose jurisdiction before creating production user objects and treat IDs as pseudonymous, not as raw user identifiers. ([Durable Object data location](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/reference/data-location/))

### D1

D1 is appropriate for rebuildable projections and global query surfaces precisely because the Usage Account remains authoritative. A single D1 database processes queries one at a time, queues bursts and eventually returns `overloaded` if its queue fills. Its immutable 10 GB/database limit, 30-second query cap, two-megabyte row/value limit, 100 bound parameters/query, six simultaneous connections per Worker invocation and 1,000 paid D1 queries per Worker invocation require indexed, bounded queries and batched migrations. Do not place unbounded audit payloads, report bytes, evidence, or raw provider webhook bodies in D1; store large bodies in R2 and index their digest/key. ([D1 limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/limits/))

D1 read replication is still public beta. Replicas are asynchronous and may be arbitrarily stale. Sequential consistency is available only through the Workers Binding Sessions API with bookmarks; calls that do not use Sessions continue to the primary. RichTide may use replication later for watchlists, public catalogs and artifact discovery, but launch correctness must work against the primary. Any read-after-write UX that opts into replicas must use `first-primary` or propagate a bookmark; balance/access checks still go to the Usage Account regardless. ([D1 global read replication](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/best-practices/read-replication/), [D1 release notes](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/release-notes/))

D1 billing is rows scanned, rows written and storage—not returned rows. Paid includes 25 billion rows read, 50 million rows written and 5 GB, then $0.001/million rows read, $1/million rows written and $0.75/GB-month. Every production query must expose `rows_read`, `rows_written` and SQL duration to cost/performance telemetry; indexes reduce scans but add writes/storage. D1 dashboard/GraphQL metrics include query volume, latency, storage and row counts. Time Travel provides minute-level point-in-time restore for 30 days on Paid, but a restore drill and an independent export/archive policy are still required. ([D1 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/), [D1 metrics](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/observability/metrics-analytics/), [D1 overview](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/))

### R2

R2 provides strong global read-after-write, metadata, delete and list consistency, so publication can safely write unique artifact objects, verify them, then publish the D1 alias. It does **not** make a key immutable: concurrent writes use last-completer-wins. Use a never-reused artifact ID plus content digest in every key, conditional writes/checks, and a manifest that lists each object's digest and size. “Latest” belongs in D1 and may be repaired; it must not overwrite canonical artifact content. If regulatory/business retention requires write-once behavior, use R2 bucket locks, which prevent overwrite and deletion for the configured period and take precedence over lifecycle deletion. ([R2 consistency](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/reference/consistency/), [bucket locks](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/buckets/bucket-locks/))

R2 objects can be up to 5 TiB (5 GiB single-part upload), with one same-key write/s. Standard pricing is $0.015/GB-month, $4.50/million Class A mutations and $0.36/million Class B reads, with no Internet egress charge. Infrequent Access adds retrieval fees and higher operation rates; it should be selected only after measured access patterns. A private R2 binding is a capability: the Worker still must authorize every requested artifact projection, and production must not use the rate-limited `r2.dev` test endpoint. ([R2 limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/platform/limits/), [R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/), [Workers R2 API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/api/workers/workers-api-reference/))

Location hints are best effort; jurisdictional restrictions guarantee storage within the chosen jurisdiction. R2, D1, Durable Objects, Containers and Worker execution must share the approved market's residency boundary before production data is created. A hint alone is insufficient for a legal residency promise. ([R2 data location](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/reference/data-location/), [Workers regional services](https://developers.cloudflare.com/data-localization/how-to/workers/))

### Workflows

JavaScript/TypeScript Workflows is GA and is the right generation orchestrator. Python Workflows remains beta, so the Workflow definition should remain TypeScript/JavaScript and invoke a Python Container. Workflows persists step output and completion, but a step body can be attempted repeatedly. Cloudflare explicitly requires API/binding effects to be idempotent. Use a stable Generation Event ID as the Workflow instance ID; use stable, versioned step names; put each external side effect behind an operation key; and return R2 references rather than evidence/report payloads. ([Workflows release notes](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/changelog/), [Rules of Workflows](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/build/rules-of-workflows/))

Default `step.do` behavior is five retries, ten-second exponential delay and ten-minute timeout per attempt; a `NonRetryableError` stops the instance after any registered rollback handlers. Configure retry limit, timeout and backoff per failure class. Schema/quality failures are terminal or human-review states, rate limits use provider-aware delay, and ambiguous writes reconcile by idempotency key before retry. Rollback is compensation, not a distributed transaction: access grants and settlements remain independent idempotent Usage Account operations. ([sleeping and retrying](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/build/sleeping-and-retrying/))

Paid Workflows currently bill Worker requests/CPU plus persisted state and steps: 10 million invocations and 30 million CPU-ms included, 1 GB state and 500,000 steps included; overage is $0.30/million invocations, $0.02/million CPU-ms, $0.20/GB-month and $0.80/100,000 steps. State for completed/errored/sleeping instances defaults to 30-day retention on Paid. Cost forecasts must include retries and retained state; delete or shorten retention only after durable business evidence is stored elsewhere. ([Workflows pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/pricing/))

Workflow metrics expose queued/start/success/failure/termination plus step attempts and rollback outcomes, retained for 31 days. Alert on queued age, failure ratio, retry exhaustion, rollback failure, step latency and state/step consumption; persist the customer-visible Generation Event state in the application model because Cloudflare's operational retention is not a customer history. ([Workflows metrics](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/observability/metrics-analytics/))

### Queues

Queues are absent from the v3 responsibility diagram despite appearing in the cost model. If retained, their correct role is narrow: a Usage Account commits an `outbox_events` row atomically with its ledger change; its idempotent alarm/dispatcher publishes only the event ID and minimal routing metadata; a Queue consumer loads/processes the event and upserts D1/notification state under the same unique ID; the Usage Account marks delivery progress after acknowledgment/reconciliation. The local outbox—not Queue order—is the replay source.

Queues deliver at least once and may duplicate; ordering is not guaranteed. Consumers therefore cannot compute balances by message order and must tolerate gaps, replay and reordering. Configure an explicit dead-letter queue, retry budget, delay, alert, and replay procedure. Queue retention is a maximum of 14 days on Paid and backlog is capped at 25 GB, so a DLQ is not an archive. A 128 KB payload maximum also argues for identifiers/R2 references, not report/evidence payloads. ([Queues delivery guarantees](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/reference/delivery-guarantees/), [Queues limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/platform/limits/))

Queue billing counts each 64 KB chunk for write/read/delete; a normal delivery is commonly three operations and each retry adds reads. Paid includes one million operations/month, then $0.40/million. Consumer invocations and CPU are also Workers usage. Metrics expose backlog, oldest-message lag, consumer concurrency, retries, outcomes and billable operations; page on age/lag, not just count. ([Queues pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/platform/pricing/), [Queues metrics](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/observability/metrics/))

### Containers

Containers are GA, available on Workers Paid, and fit Python/pandas calculations that exceed the Worker runtime's language, memory or CPU shape. Current predefined sizes run from `lite` (1/16 vCPU, 256 MiB, 2 GB disk) through `standard-4` (4 vCPU, 12 GiB, 20 GB disk); account defaults allow 6 TiB concurrent memory, 1,500 vCPU, 30 TB disk and 50 GB stored images. These are sufficient in principle, but the production image and each analysis method must be benchmarked against the selected instance type and concurrency cap. ([Containers overview](https://developers.cloudflare.com/containers/), [Containers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/containers/platform-details/limits/))

All local disk is ephemeral. A stopped/sleeping container restarts with a fresh image disk and may move location. Cold starts are commonly 1–3 seconds and depend on image/entrypoint. Persist inputs/outputs/checkpoints to R2 and keep all job/lease state in Workflow/Coordinator storage; handle `SIGTERM` and assume restart at every method boundary. ([Container lifecycle](https://developers.cloudflare.com/containers/platform-details/architecture/))

By default a Container allows Internet access. Set `enableInternet = false`; allow only approved virtual hosts; route market-data, R2/D1 and any provider calls through Worker outbound handlers; keep credentials in Worker secrets so they never enter the image/container; and enable HTTPS interception only with the documented CA trust setup. End users reach Containers only through Worker HTTP routing, but this alone is not an egress or capability boundary. ([outbound traffic](https://developers.cloudflare.com/containers/platform-details/outbound-traffic/), [Workers/bindings connections](https://developers.cloudflare.com/containers/platform-details/workers-connections/))

Container placement can be restricted by region; `eu` and `fedramp` jurisdiction constraints are documented, while Middle East, Oceania and Africa have limited capacity and cannot be the sole regional constraint without dedicated access. Confirm the chosen launch jurisdiction is supported across all primitives. ([Container placement](https://developers.cloudflare.com/containers/platform-details/placement/))

Container deployment is explicitly non-transactional: Worker code activates before image upload/rollout completes; old container images can remain reachable during rollout; “deploy success” means rollout started, not finished. Define a versioned RPC/HTTP contract with backward and forward compatibility, health/readiness checks, a non-zero active grace period where needed, post-deploy rollout verification, and rollback drills. ([Container rollouts](https://developers.cloudflare.com/containers/platform-details/rollouts/))

Containers bill provisioned memory and disk while running plus active CPU, in 10 ms increments. Paid includes 25 GiB-hours memory, 375 vCPU-minutes and 200 GB-hours disk; current overages are $0.0000025/GiB-second, $0.000020/vCPU-second and $0.00000007/GB-second. Logs use Workers Logs pricing. Forecast cold-start retries, idle `sleepAfter`, parallel Pro methods, provisioned memory/disk and active CPU separately. ([Containers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/containers/pricing/))

## Required architecture corrections

1. **Replace “Workflows: idempotency” with “Workflows: durable retries around application-owned idempotent operations.”** No Workflow, Queue or alarm retry may create a second generation charge, provider effect, publication, access grant or settlement.
2. **Assign Queues explicitly or remove them.** Recommended: use a Queue only for D1 projections, notifications and reconciliation work emitted from Durable Object local outboxes. Add the outbox dispatcher, DLQ, replay path and projection cursor to the architecture diagram. Do not insert Queue between a customer request and authoritative reservation/grant/settlement response.
3. **Make artifact publication a replay-safe protocol:** write unique R2 component keys → verify digests/schema → write manifest → upsert D1 artifact row/alias under unique generation/version keys → notify Coordinator → independently grant/settle requesters. An orphaned R2 artifact or delayed D1 alias must be repairable without regeneration or recharge.
4. **Change “R2 immutable artifacts” to “R2 artifacts immutable by unique keys, conditional-write rules and retention policy.”** Separate raw snapshot retention, published artifact retention, legal holds and correction/supersession; never overwrite a published artifact to correct it.
5. **Seal the Container boundary:** `enableInternet=false`, explicit outbound virtual-host allowlist/handlers, no provider/API secrets in container env, no canonical state on disk, digest-pinned image/SBOM, resource and timeout limits, `SIGTERM` handling, and versioned Worker↔Container contract.
6. **Keep the Workflow in JavaScript/TypeScript.** Python is confined to the GA Container platform; Python Workflows beta is not a launch dependency.
7. **Make D1 partitionable.** Define an initial database layout and measurable triggers for separating high-write provider/audit events, catalogs, analysis indexes, and user projections. Store bulky payloads in R2. No design may assume a future increase above 10 GB/database.
8. **Define residency as an end-to-end deployment property.** Create production D1 databases, Durable Object namespaces, R2 buckets and Container constraints in the approved jurisdiction before ingesting data; configure Worker Regional Services where required. Ordinary location hints are latency hints, not legal guarantees.
9. **Separate Cloudflare operational telemetry from business evidence.** Every request/generation/provider event gets a non-secret correlation ID propagated through Worker, Workflow, Container, R2 manifest, D1 index, Queue and Durable Object outbox. Business audit facts persist under the approved retention policy; sampled/seven-day platform logs are supplemental.
10. **Add capacity and spend controls.** Pin per-service CPU/resource limits, Workflow/Container/Queue concurrency, Queue backlog alarms, D1 scan budgets, R2 lifecycle/lock policy, log sampling/redaction and monthly service budgets. The product's Analysis Credit is a commercial abstraction, not an assumption that infrastructure cost is fixed.

## Explicit production qualification gates

All gates are fail-closed and must be evidenced in staging against deployed Cloudflare resources, not only local emulators.

### Gate CF-1 — Account, status and configuration inventory

- Workers Paid is active in the owning account; immutable deployed version IDs and bindings are recorded.
- Current product status is rechecked: no launch-critical dependency uses D1 read-replication beta, Python Workflows beta, Workers tracing beta or OTel-export beta.
- Wrangler configurations pin compatibility dates, resource/CPU limits, queue retry/DLQ settings, Workflow state retention, container instance type/max instances/placement/rollout policy, and observability sampling.
- A generated infrastructure inventory proves every environment's actual bindings; top-level configuration is not assumed to inherit into named environments.

### Gate CF-2 — Authority and atomicity proof

- A state-machine/concurrency test proves the Usage Account is the only balance/access authority and that D1 unavailability/staleness cannot authorize spend or delivery.
- Property/concurrency tests prove grant lots never go negative; a reservation, provider effect, access transition and settlement apply at most once; ledger/state/outbox commit together.
- Every mutating DO RPC, Workflow step, alarm handler, Queue consumer, provider webhook and publication operation has a stable idempotency key and unique constraint.
- Fault injection at every external-call boundary proves replay, ambiguous-timeout reconciliation and independent requester settlement without double charge or cross-user rollback.

### Gate CF-3 — Durable execution and recovery

- The Generation Event uses a deterministic Workflow instance ID and versioned stable step names.
- Retry/timeout/`NonRetryableError` policies are classified per step; large results are R2 references, not Workflow state.
- Tests cover Worker disconnect, Workflow retry exhaustion, pause/resume/restart, Container crash/SIGTERM, quality block, partial Core publication, D1 failure after R2 write, Coordinator eviction and requester entitlement revocation.
- Recovery can adopt/repair an orphaned artifact and resume requester fan-out without regeneration or recharge.

### Gate CF-4 — Queue/outbox recovery

- If Queues is retained, its documented sole responsibilities, message schema and retention are approved; otherwise it is removed from the architecture and cost model.
- Duplicate, reordered, delayed, expired and DLQ messages reproduce the same D1/notification projection as a single in-order delivery.
- The Durable Object outbox can republish after Queue outage beyond the alarm's six automatic retries and after Queue's maximum retention; DLQ replay is rehearsed.
- Alerts cover oldest-message age, backlog bytes/count, retries, failed/DLQ outcomes and projection-cursor drift.

### Gate CF-5 — Storage, consistency and disaster recovery

- R2 artifact writes use unique/content-addressed keys, digest verification and conditional creation; bucket privacy, jurisdiction, lifecycle and lock rules are inspected live.
- D1 schemas are indexed and all representative queries remain below agreed p95 SQL duration and rows-scanned budgets under peak concurrency; database-size forecasts define archive/partition thresholds well below 10 GB.
- D1 Time Travel restore is rehearsed and projections are rebuilt from Durable Object outbox/export plus provider reconciliation.
- An R2/D1/Durable Object residency matrix proves all customer data stays in the approved jurisdiction; known metadata/log exceptions are accepted by governance.

### Gate CF-6 — Container security, compatibility and capacity

- Image is digest-pinned, reproducible, vulnerability-scanned and has an SBOM; it contains no credentials or customer data.
- Live configuration proves Internet egress is disabled by default and only approved outbound handlers/hosts are reachable; attempts to reach arbitrary HTTP, HTTPS, DNS exfiltration paths and non-80/443 ports fail as designed.
- Restart from fresh disk produces identical results from R2 inputs; cold-start, memory, disk, CPU, concurrency and scale-to-zero behavior meet SLO and cost budgets for Core and parallel Pro loads.
- A mixed-version rollout test proves old/new Worker code and old/new images interoperate; readiness, rollout completion and rollback are verified separately from `wrangler deploy` success.

### Gate CF-7 — Observability, privacy and cost

- One correlation ID follows a synthetic job across Worker, Workflow, Container, R2 manifest, D1 rows, Coordinator/Usage Account logs and Queue/outbox; no secret, raw authorization header, model credential, or unnecessary PII appears.
- Dashboards/alerts cover Workers resource failures, DO overload/storage, Workflow queue/retries/rollback, Container exit/cold start/resource use, Queue lag/DLQ, D1 latency/rows/storage and R2 operations/storage.
- Business audit/reconciliation history is recoverable beyond Cloudflare log/metric retention and does not depend on sampled traces.
- A measured staging load and failure run populates the cost model for Worker requests/CPU/logs, DO requests/duration/rows/storage, Workflow invocations/CPU/steps/state, Queue operations/retries, D1 scans/writes/storage, R2 operations/storage/retrieval and Container CPU/memory/disk. Budget alerts and kill switches are exercised.

### Gate CF-8 — Regional production readiness

- The chosen launch market is supported by the required D1, Durable Object, R2, Container and Worker regional controls; limited-capacity Container regions are not assumed available exclusively.
- From target customer geographies, p95/p99 latency is measured for Worker→Usage Account, Worker→D1, Workflow→Container and private artifact delivery; first-request placement does not accidentally pin long-lived user objects to an operational/admin location.
- Cloudflare service-degradation runbooks define customer-visible behavior: no silent charge, no access loss from stale D1, no duplicate generation, and truthful queued/partial/degraded states.

## Decision for downstream tickets

Proceed with the Cloudflare-native design after incorporating the ten corrections above. The downstream orchestration, usage-state, artifact and frontend architecture tickets should treat this document's authority matrix and gates as constraints. No new infrastructure vendor is required by current limits. Production approval remains blocked until the launch jurisdiction is chosen and Gates CF-1 through CF-8 have deployed evidence.
